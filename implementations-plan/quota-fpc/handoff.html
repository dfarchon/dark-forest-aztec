Gasless onboarding for Dark Forest

A fee-paying contract your app deploys and funds. It covers each player's transaction fees — up to a daily allowance, and only for Dark Forest's own contracts — so a new player can start moving without first bridging any fee juice. No changes to your deployed game contracts are required.

Why this exists

Today a new player can't do anything until they bridge $AZTEC into fee juice. That wall is where most curious players drop off. This removes it: the app holds a small pool of fee juice, and pays on the player's behalf. The player just plays; when their daily allowance runs out, the game falls back to the normal path (their own gas, or the bridge).

How it works — the "sandwich"

Aztec's standard fee-payment contracts can't see what a transaction does, so they'd sponsor anything. To restrict sponsorship to Dark Forest, this paymaster becomes the transaction's entry point and wraps around the player's own account:

  1. The paymaster is the transaction origin. It checks every call in the player-signed payload targets an allowlisted Dark Forest contract, and that the player is running approved account software that cannot swap its own code.
  2. It records the player's daily allowance (one subscription per player per day, a per-day capacity cap), and asserts the fee is within its per-transaction ceiling.
  3. It elects itself fee payer and ends setup.
  4. It hands off to the player's own account, which verifies the player's signature and runs the calls — so the game still sees the player as msg_sender. Ownership checks keep working, untouched.

The daily reset is pure chain-time arithmetic (UTC day index) — there's no cron and no scheduled job. Whoever deploys the paymaster also holds a key that can retune it; what that key can and cannot do is spelled out below.

That one session is the last gap, and it is also what will give the true per-move cost — expect it above the 0.85 figure below, since a real contract does more work.

Assumptions — per-transaction cost & token price

Measured by playing Dark Forest on Aztec mainnet, 1 August 2026 — real moves, sponsored, paid by the paymaster. A move costs 6.58 fee juice, spawning a player 1.89, claiming spaceships 4.60. The five sponsored actions of that session cost 26.22 in total, which matches the paymaster's balance to four decimal places.

Note how much the action matters. An earlier draft of this page used 0.85 — the cost of a bare sponsored transaction against a do-nothing contract. That is the paymaster's own overhead, not the price of playing, and it understates a real move by almost eight times. Budget from the action you expect players to perform, not from the sponsorship machinery. (An even earlier draft used a local-testnet figure that was ~888,000× too low.)

The worst case — a transaction using every bit of gas the client permits — is 20.24, and the paymaster refuses anything above 25. Fees float with demand, so re-measure before committing a large budget.

Update if the token price has moved since this was written.

Two numbers are easy to confuse. The ceiling (25) is what the paymaster refuses to exceed on any single transaction; the cost (about 0.85) is what one actually settles for. The gap is deliberate: the ceiling is charged against gas limits, the fee against gas used. So the worst case for this policy — 2 players × 5 transactions, ×3 for the daily-rollover window — is 750 at the ceiling but nearer 26 in practice.

Re-measure before a large budget

Operating it

Deploy

One command, reading the config. It prints the whole plan (policy, worst-case daily spend, loss cap, every sponsored contract) and refuses any policy whose worst case exceeds the accepted loss:

pnpm --filter contracts run deploy-fpc -- --config fpc/config/dark-forest.json

Fund — and refuel

Refueling never needs a redeploy or any special call. Anyone bridges fee juice to the paymaster's address, from any account, at any time. Its balance goes up and it keeps sponsoring. Do this whenever it runs low.

Fund above the reserve, or nothing works

A sponsored transaction settles for well under one $AZTEC, but the network will not accept it unless the paymaster already holds the worst case that transaction could possibly cost — currently about 21. Fund it with less than that and it sponsors precisely nothing, while looking perfectly healthy: the balance is positive, the policy is correct, and every player is quietly billed their own gas. The error only appears at submission, as "Insufficient fee payer balance", long after the funding decision.

So there is a floor, not just a budget. --show now prints it and says plainly whether the current balance clears it. Keep a comfortable multiple above it — the reserve is re-checked per transaction and rises with network fees.

Change the quotas (more players, more transactions)

One command, and no redeploy:

pnpm --filter contracts run update-fpc-policy -- --fpc <address> --show
pnpm --filter contracts run update-fpc-policy -- --fpc <address> --max-uses 50

--show tells you what is live now, what change is already queued and exactly when it lands. Any change takes effect twelve hours later — that delay is fixed in the contract and nobody, including the key holder, can shorten it. It exists so a change is always announced before it binds.

Two things the tool refuses, both of which are quiet disasters otherwise: overwriting a change you already have queued (only one can be pending at a time — a second silently replaces the first), and setting a spending ceiling so low that every sponsored transaction fails and players get billed their own gas.

A reduction binds everyone the moment it lands, including players already midway through their day: their remaining count can drop, and anyone holding a slot above the new player limit stops being sponsored. Increases take nothing away from anyone, and reach players already midway through their day as well as new ones.

Irreversible

Fee juice sent to a paymaster cannot be withdrawn — the protocol makes it non-transferable, which is why not even the key holder can take it out. Retuning no longer strands anything, since the numbers change in place. But the balance is still the real, hard cap on what you can lose, and it is the only cap: the key can raise the spending limits, and there is no way to pull funds back. So fund in tranches — deposit what you would accept losing, and top up as it proves out.

Honest caveats

This was built and proven end-to-end against your real contracts on a local network — a player with zero fee juice gets past onboarding and plays, sponsored. Two things you should know before scaling funding beyond a showcase:

The restriction now holds for everyone

Earlier this was only true for ordinary accounts — someone using a custom account contract could spend their own daily allowance elsewhere. That is closed. The paymaster now also checks which account software the player is running: only the wallet type your players actually use is sponsored, and it must be an account that cannot later swap its own code. Both halves matter — the first version of this check was defeated by an account upgrading itself after approval, which a security review caught before it shipped.

Somebody holds a key

Whoever deploys the paymaster can afterwards change the numbers and the list of sponsored contracts. The address is fixed at deployment and cannot be transferred — so if you want to hold it, you should be the one who deploys. In our showcase deployment it is our own deploy account, on the machine we build from.

What the key cannot do: take money out (the protocol makes deposited fee juice non-transferable, for everyone including us), shorten the twelve-hour notice period, or touch the wallet-security rules described above.

What it can do: raise the spending limits, and point sponsorship at different contracts. So the honest framing is not "your funds could be stolen" but "sponsorship could be redirected until the balance runs down". Two consequences follow: the funding cap in our config is a sanity check rather than an enforced limit, and only ever deposit what you would accept losing.

There is no emergency stop. Nothing takes effect in under twelve hours — not a spending cut, not removing a contract from the list. If something goes wrong, the earliest any fix lands is half a day later. Funding in small tranches is the protection; reacting quickly is not available.

What that costs you

Sponsorship is limited to players on the standard embedded wallet. Two consequences worth knowing: upgrading the account library changes its identifier, so players on the new version need a paymaster redeploy to stay sponsored; and anyone who knows a player's account details can publish it on-chain, which permanently ends sponsorship for that player. That is a nuisance aimed at one person, not a way to take funds — but it is unfixable once done.

Gas is estimated, not yet measured on your network

The per-transaction gas limits are conservative estimates. A heavier-than-expected call could exceed them; if that happens at execution time the transaction reverts, the paymaster still pays, and the player loses one sponsored transaction. Calibrating against real transactions on the target network (a script exists for this) pins the ceiling before funding.

What's solid

Seven independent security reviews. The allowance accounting is exact, the daily reset needs no operator, and a reverting game move is handled correctly (it consumes one use rather than stranding the player mid-day). The whole thing degrades safely: if sponsorship is ever unavailable, players simply fall back to their own gas. Every claim on this page is backed by tests that run against the real contracts on a live network — including one proving the account-upgrade route is blocked, and one that fast-forwards the clock to prove a queued change really is inert until its time.

What you'd do to run it

  • Decide the funding tranche (start small — it's unrecoverable) and the quota numbers.
  • Deploy the paymaster against the game contracts, calibrate the gas ceiling, fund the first tranche.
  • Set VITE_QUOTA_FPC_ADDRESS in the client build — that single switch turns sponsorship on; without it the client behaves exactly as today.
  • Watch usage; refuel as needed; change the numbers with one command when you want to (twelve hours' notice, no redeploy).
