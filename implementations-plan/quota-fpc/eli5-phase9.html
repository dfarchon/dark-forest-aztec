Dark Forest paymaster · Phase 9 · for approval

Turning the dials without redeploying

The paymaster's settings are welded shut at deploy: how many players it sponsors a day, how many free transactions each gets, what it will spend per transaction, and which game contracts count. Changing any of them today means deploying a new one and abandoning the money left in the old. This phase puts those four settings behind a timer you can turn — agree the numbers, send one transaction, wait twelve hours, done.

Why it needs a timer at all

Aztec proves transactions privately and in advance, against a snapshot of the world. If a setting could change at any instant, a proof built moments ago might rest on a number that has already moved. So changes are announced first and take effect later — everyone's in-flight proofs stay valid because the change was visible before it landed. Twelve hours is the window you chose.

The thing to understand before approving

Nothing takes effect in under twelve hours, and there is no emergency stop. If something goes wrong — a defect, abuse, a fee spike — the earliest any change you make can land is half a day later. That is the deal this design makes in exchange for private transactions staying valid. The real protection is not reacting quickly; it is only ever funding the paymaster with an amount you would accept losing.

What changes for players

You chose that a cut applies to everyone the moment it lands, rather than letting people who already started the day finish on the old numbers. That keeps the sums honest — the amount at risk is always the current settings, nothing inherited — but it has a visible cost:

  • A player's remaining free transactions can drop mid-session when a reduction takes effect.
  • A player who was being sponsored can lose sponsorship mid-day if you cut the number of sponsored players below their slot.

Neither is a bug, and the wording players see has to say so. Raising the numbers has no such effect — more is always simply more.

What it costs you

What you gainWhat you give up
Retune in one transaction instead of a redeploy. No stranded money, no client rebuild, no players split across two paymasters. The paymaster stops being a thing nobody controls. Someone holds a key that can change the settings and the sponsored-contract list.
The Dark Forest team can tune it themselves, if they deploy it themselves — whoever deploys holds the key permanently. The funding cap in the config becomes a sanity check rather than a limit, because the key can raise the settings past it later.
Every change is announced twelve hours ahead and publicly visible, so nothing happens quietly. That visibility cuts both ways: a scheduled increase is announced to everyone, including anyone waiting to take advantage of it.

The key cannot take money out — the paymaster has no withdrawal, by protocol. The risk is redirection, not theft: sponsorship could be pointed somewhere else until the balance runs down.

The work, in five steps

1 · The contract

Put the four settings behind the timer, add the key that is allowed to change them, and make sure the paymaster works from the moment it is deployed rather than waking up twelve hours later.

Proves itself by: the contracts compile and the generated code shows the new controls.

2 · Configuration and deployment

The config file names who holds the key — explicitly, never guessed. Deployment prints who that is and states plainly that the funding cap is now advice rather than a limit.

Proves itself by: tests pass and a rehearsal deployment prints the right warnings without sending anything.

3 · The tool for changing settings

One command, meant for someone who has never seen this code. It shows what the settings are now, what change is already queued and when it lands, and what the new numbers would cost against the money actually in the paymaster. It refuses to quietly overwrite a queued change, and refuses a spending limit so low it would break every sponsored transaction.

Proves itself by: running it against a live test network and confirming it refuses the things it should refuse.

4 · Tests

Nine checks against a real network, including the two that matter most: that a change genuinely is not active before its time and is after, and that the paymaster works immediately on deployment. Time is fast-forwarded so this takes minutes rather than twelve hours.

Proves itself by: all nine green, none skipped. This is the finish line.

5 · Documentation and the handoff

The page written for the Dark Forest team currently says there is no key and nothing is privileged. That becomes false the moment this ships, so it gets rewritten honestly — including where the key lives.

Proves itself by: a search across the repo turns up no surviving claim that there is no key.

How this plan was checked

Three independent review rounds, each of which found something real:

  • Round 1 caught that a twelve-hour timer would have left the paymaster sponsoring nothing for its first twelve hours after every deployment — silently — and that my safety check against overwriting a queued change was cosmetic, since two people could pass it at once.
  • Round 2 caught that I had told you the spending limit could be lowered instantly for emergencies. It cannot. Nothing can.
  • Round 3 caught that my formula for cutting allowances handed people one transaction more than intended, and that cutting the player count did not actually stop players already admitted.

All are fixed. A fourth round was deliberately skipped: what remained were inconsistencies in my own writing rather than flaws in the design, and reviews find more once there is code to read.

If this goes wrong

Everything built so far is committed and green at a known-good point. If this phase turns out badly, one command returns the project to a paymaster that works, is fully tested, and simply cannot be retuned. Nothing here is a one-way door.
